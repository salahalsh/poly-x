# Systematic comparison with existing polymer-informatics platforms

Prepared for Reviewer 1 ("The comparison to existing tools is also incomplete")
and Reviewer 2, Minor 13 ("A concise table should compare existing public tools
by supported properties, representation, validation protocol, applicability-
domain support, uncertainty estimates, interpretability, code availability, and
deployment status"). Facts verified 2026-09-03 from the primary publications
and repositories cited below; the search was restricted to tools that are
publicly usable or whose code is public, and it is not claimed to be exhaustive.

## Corrections to the submitted manuscript that this search established

1. **polyBERT pre-training corpus.** The submitted manuscript (Section 2.4,
   line 199) states polyBERT was "Pre-trained on approximately one million
   polymer SMILES strings from the PI1M dataset". This is wrong on both count
   and provenance. Kuenneth and Ramprasad pre-trained polyBERT on **100 million
   hypothetical PSMILES**, generated by decomposing 13,766 known polymers into
   4,424 BRICS fragments and recombining them. PI1M is a different, ~1 million
   structure dataset (Ma and Luo) and was not the polyBERT pre-training corpus.
   *Reviewer 2, Major 2 is correct.*

2. **The polyBERT fingerprint is mean-pooled, not CLS.** The published
   fingerprint is "the average over the token dimension (sentence average) of
   the last Transformer encoder"; the submitted Tier 3 used the final-layer CLS
   vector. *Reviewer 2, Major 2 is correct.*

3. **Canonicalisation is integral to polyBERT.** The polyBERT authors shipped a
   dedicated `psmiles` package because "polyBERT requires canonicalized PSMILES
   strings" - the fingerprint varies with the PSMILES writing, unlike
   handcrafted fingerprints. Our own measurement (script 03) reproduces this:
   over 200 polymers, re-writing the same repeat unit changes the CLS cosine to
   as little as 0.81, and writing the polymer as two concatenated repeat units
   changes it to a mean of 0.73 (min 0.13). ECFP4 is markedly more robust to
   the same perturbation (mean Tanimoto 0.84). *Reviewer 2, Major 7 is correct.*

4. **PolyMetriX curation.** The source collection is 7,367 unique
   canonicalised PSMILES-Tg pairs aggregated from nine sources; the submitted
   manuscript reported 7,365 after "cleaning". Script 01 identifies the two
   removed records exactly (see below). *Reviewer 2, Minor 1 is answered.*

## The two removed records (Reviewer 2, Minor 1)

Both were removed by the submitted pipeline's global 3-sigma filter on Tg
(mean 417.06 K, SD 112.70 K, upper bound 755.18 K); no record was removed by
deduplication, because the source collection is already deduplicated:

| PSMILES | Tg (K) | Family |
|---|---|---|
| `[*]c1cc2c(cc1C)-c1cc(C)c(N3C(=O)c4ccc(-c5ccc6c(c5)C(=O)N([*])C6=O)cc4C3=O)cc1S2(=O)=O` | 763.15 | polyimide/sulfone |
| `[*]c1cccc(-c2nc3ccc(-c4ccc5nc([*])sc5c4)cc3s2)c1` | 768.15 | polybenzothiazole |

Both are high-performance heteroaromatic polymers at the top of the Tg range.
Removing the two highest-Tg records is a target-dependent operation applied
before splitting, so information from the eventual test partition influenced
preprocessing. In the revision the filter is fitted on the training partition
only.

## What this comparison implies for the novelty claim

Both referees are right that no individual component is new. Group
contribution, Morgan fingerprints with tree ensembles, transformer embeddings,
Tanimoto applicability domains and hosted prediction services all pre-date this
work. Two things in the table are, to our reading, not currently offered
together anywhere else, and these are the only claims the revision should make:

1. a single free interface in which a physics-based, a fingerprint-based and a
   transformer-based estimate for the *same* query are returned side by side
   with their disagreement made explicit; and
2. per-prediction reliability that has been **calibrated against observed
   error** and reported with measured interval coverage, rather than asserted.

Neither is a new prediction methodology. The revised abstract, introduction
and conclusions should say so explicitly and should describe POLY-X as a
usability- and reliability-oriented integration, which is what Reviewer 1 asked
for.
